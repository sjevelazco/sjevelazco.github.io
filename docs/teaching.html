Teaching & Mentoring

Philosophy

I see teaching and mentoring as a shared construction of knowledge rather than a one-way transfer. Students bring their own experiences, questions and realities to the classroom, and learning works best when it starts from them and leads to critical, independent thinking.

In my group I favor teamwork over individual competition: students help each other and draw on each person’s different abilities. We meet weekly or biweekly to discuss progress, calibrate ideas and solve problems together, and I give the most support to those who need it most. Mentoring is, for me, the best way to build critical reasoning, organization and collaboration skills — and to explore new ideas.

A core principle of my mentoring is that theses and dissertations should become peer-reviewed papers. Around 90% of my mentees have published their thesis work, which strengthens their careers, benefits their graduate programs, and returns the results of publicly funded research to society.

I have taught and mentored in more than six graduate and undergraduate programs, across very different social and educational contexts in Argentina, Brazil, and beyond.
